THE KNOWLEDGE DESK

Useful context for better questions.

Short, general information to help visitors prepare for a conversation. These articles are illustrative website content and are not legal advice.

Before signing a business agreement

＋

A written agreement should make the working relationship easier to understand, not harder. Before signing, take time to identify the people or organisations involved and the purpose of the arrangement.

Questions worth organising

  • Are the parties and their roles described correctly?
  • Does the document explain the work, goods or services being provided?
  • Are payment terms, dates and responsibilities clear?
  • Does it explain how changes, delays or disagreements will be handled?
  • Do you understand how the arrangement can end?

If a term is unclear, note the question and ask an appropriately qualified professional to explain its effect in the context of the full document.

Preparing for a property transaction

＋

Property transactions involve several people, documents and important dates. Having the basic information organised can make an initial conversation more focused.

A useful starting file

  • The names of the parties involved and the property details available to you.
  • Any draft offer, sale agreement or related correspondence.
  • Questions about timing, conditions, finance or transfer steps.
  • Known dates or deadlines connected to the transaction.
  • Details of other professionals already involved, where relevant.

Do not assume a general online explanation applies to your transaction. The documents and circumstances should be reviewed by the appropriate professional.

Making your first legal conversation useful

＋

You do not need to know every legal term before speaking with a legal professional. A clear outline of what happened, what you are concerned about and what you hope to understand is a useful place to begin.

Consider preparing

  • A short timeline of the main events.
  • The key people or organisations involved.
  • Any important dates you are aware of.
  • The main documents you have, without sending sensitive copies through an unapproved channel.
  • The questions you most want answered.

A first enquiry is not the same as a formal appointment or an agreement for representation. The firm must confirm whether it can assist and explain the next steps.

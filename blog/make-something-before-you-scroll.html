Make something before you scroll.

Lower the starting line

You do not need a brilliant idea to begin. Draw an object on the table, write four lines about yesterday or arrange three photographs into a sequence. Pick an action small enough that you can complete it before negotiating with yourself.

The first version belongs to you. It does not need to become a post, product or portfolio piece. When every experiment has to perform in public, it becomes harder to try something awkward. Keep a place where unfinished work is welcome.

Borrow a boundary

Ten minutes, one colour or a single paragraph can be a helpful limit. Constraints reduce the number of decisions waiting at the beginning. They also give you permission to stop while you still have energy to return.

Keep the tools visible and ready. An open notebook asks less of you than a box you have to unpack. If mornings are impossible, choose another moment before your usual scrolling session. The order matters more than the hour.

Collect evidence of showing up

Put a small date on what you made. After a week, look at the collection without ranking it. Which task was easiest to begin? Which one left you curious? These are more useful questions than whether the work is impressive.

The aim is not to turn every spare minute into output. It is to give your own attention a little space before borrowing everyone else’s. Some days the result will be a page. Other days it will be an idea you finally allowed to exist.
